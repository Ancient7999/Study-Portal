# Axial Skeleton

---

## Definition

The **axial skeleton** is the central axis of the body: skull, hyoid, vertebral column, and thoracic cage. It forms a protective case for the brain, spinal cord, heart, and lungs, and provides attachment for muscles of the head, neck, and trunk.

**Textbook count: 80 bones.**

## Breakdown

| Region | Bones | Count |
|--------|-------|------:|
| Cranial | Frontal, parietal ×2, temporal ×2, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid | 8 |
| Facial | Maxilla ×2, zygomatic ×2, nasal ×2, lacrimal ×2, palatine ×2, inferior nasal concha ×2, vomer, mandible | 14 |
| Auditory ossicles | Malleus ×2, incus ×2, stapes ×2 | 6 |
| Neck | Hyoid | 1 |
| Vertebral column | Cervical 7 + thoracic 12 + lumbar 5 + sacrum + coccyx | 26 |
| Thoracic cage | Ribs 24 + sternum | 25 |
| **Total** | | **80** |

## Key points

- The **skull** = 8 cranial + 14 facial = 22 bones (ossicles and hyoid are listed separately).
- The **vertebral column** counts the sacrum and coccyx as one bone each in the adult (fused vertebrae).
- Ribs are **24** (12 pairs). The sternum is one flat bone (manubrium, body, and xiphoid process are regions, not separate bones in the adult count).
- Shape filter tip: most vertebrae and many facial bones are **irregular**; cranial vault bones, ribs, and sternum are **flat**.
