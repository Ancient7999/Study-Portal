# White Blood Cells

---

## Definition

**White blood cells (WBC)**, or **leukocytes** (leuk/o = white, -cyte = cell), are the formed elements of blood that defend the body against infection. They divide into two classes by whether their cytoplasm holds visible **granules**:

| Class | Nucleus | Granules | Members |
|-------|---------|----------|---------|
| **Granulocytes** (polymorphonuclear) | Lobed (many-lobed) | Yes, visible after staining | Neutrophil, Eosinophil, Basophil |
| **Agranulocytes** | Single, non-lobed | None | Monocyte, Lymphocyte |

**Polymorphonuclear:** poly = many, morph/o = form, nucle/o = kernel (nucleus), so a many-lobed nucleus.

**Granule:** a tiny sac in the cytoplasm that holds enzymes and other chemicals and takes up stain, which is how granulocytes are told apart.

**Phagocytosis:** a cell engulfing and digesting particles such as bacteria or dead cells (phag/o = eat, cyt/o = cell, -osis = process).

## WBC tree

Granulocytes are amber, agranulocytes are teal, and the lymphocyte subtypes are violet.

## Summary table

| Cell | Group | Lifespan | Stain | Function |
|------|-------|----------|-------|----------|
| **Neutrophil** | Granulocyte | About 24 hours | Takes up neither stain (neutral) | Phagocytosis; fights bacteria; most common WBC |
| **Eosinophil** | Granulocyte | 2–5 days | Bright yellow-red to orange (rosy), with eosin | Increases during allergy and parasitic infection |
| **Basophil** | Granulocyte | Under 2 days | Dark blue to blue-black | Immune surveillance; brings anticoagulants to inflamed tissue |
| **Monocyte** | Agranulocyte | Up to 24 hours | No granules to stain | Largest WBC; cleans up dead cells and germs (viruses, bacteria, fungi, protozoa) by phagocytosis |
| **Lymphocyte** | Agranulocyte | See subtypes | No granules to stain | One large nucleus; makes antibodies; gives rise to T, B and NK cells |
| **T-cell** | Lymphocyte | 30–160 days | — | Thymus-derived; attacks viruses, cancer cells and foreign cells |
| **B-cell** | Lymphocyte | 5–6 weeks | — | Arises from bone marrow; makes antibodies |
| **NK cell** | Lymphocyte | Up to 2 weeks | — | Natural killer; carries cytotoxic substances that kill tumor and infected cells |

**Stain word parts:** eosin/o = red / orange / rosy (eosinophil); neutr/o = neither (neutrophil); bas/o = base (basophil); -phil = attraction for.

## Related formed elements

| Element | Other name | Lifespan | Role |
|---------|------------|----------|------|
| **Red blood cell (RBC)** | Erythrocyte | About 110 to 120 days | Contains **hemoglobin** (protein + iron compound) and carries oxygen and carbon dioxide |
| **Platelet** | Thrombocyte | About 7 to 10 days | Fragment shed by the **megakaryocyte** (in bone marrow); essential for **coagulation** (blood clotting) |

<div class="circ-extra">
<p><span class="note-badge grey" title="How the values above compare with standard references">Notes On Values</span></p>
<p><strong>Megakaryocytes 7–10 days.</strong> Standard references usually give 7–10 days for <strong>platelets (thrombocytes)</strong>, the cell fragments that megakaryocytes shed; the quiz pairs 7–10 days with the thrombocyte.</p>
<p><strong>Monocytes up to 24 hours.</strong> Standard references describe monocytes circulating for about 1–3 days before entering the tissues as macrophages, which can live much longer. The course value is kept here.</p>
<p><strong>Lymphocytes make antibodies.</strong> Within the lymphocyte line, antibodies come from <strong>B-cells</strong>; T-cells and NK cells attack cells directly.</p>
<p><strong>T-cell and B-cell lifespans.</strong> Memory T- and B-cells can persist for years; the ranges above are the course values.</p>
</div>
