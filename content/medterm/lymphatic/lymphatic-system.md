# Lymphatic System

---

## Definition

The **lymphatic system** is a network of **lymph vessels, lymph nodes and lymphoid organs** that collects excess tissue fluid, filters it, and returns it to the bloodstream, while housing the **lymphocytes** of the immune system.

**Lymph/o** = clear fluid (lymph), as in **lymphoduct**, a duct carrying lymph.

## Main parts

| Part | What it is | Key facts |
|------|------------|-----------|
| **Lymph** | Colorless, watery fluid collected from the body tissues | **Interstitial fluid** bathes cells outside vessels; once it enters lymphatic capillaries it is called **lymph** |
| **Lymph vessels (lymphatics)** | Lymphatic capillaries → larger vessels → lymphatic ducts | Capillaries collect interstitial fluid; **valves** stop lymph flowing back to the tissues |
| **Lymph nodes** | Small bean-shaped filters along the vessels | Filter **microorganisms and particles** from lymph; lymph enters by **afferent** vessels and leaves by **efferent** vessels |

### Lymphatic ducts

| Duct | Drains | Empties into |
|------|--------|--------------|
| **Thoracic duct** | Lower body and **left** upper body (left head, neck, chest, arm) | Left subclavian vein |
| **Right lymphatic duct** | **Right** side of the head, neck and chest (and right arm) | Right subclavian vein |

## Organs of the lymphatic system

| Organ | Location | Role |
|-------|----------|------|
| **Red bone marrow** | Inside bones | Produces **red cells, white cells and platelets**; **B-cells** arise here (primary lymphoid organ) |
| **Thymus** | Upper chest, behind the sternum | Produces **T-lymphocytes** that fight off foreign organisms (primary lymphoid organ) |
| **Spleen** | Upper left abdomen | Filters and removes **old and damaged blood cells**; fights infection and disease |
| **Lymph nodes** | Along lymph vessels (neck, armpit, groin, abdomen) | Filter microorganisms and particles from lymph |
| **Tonsils and adenoid** | Throat and back of the nose | Trap pathogens from **food and air**: a first line of defense |
| **Peyer's patches** | Lining of the **small intestine** | Monitor and destroy bacteria |
| **Appendix** | Off the large intestine | Houses **good (beneficial) bacteria** for the gut |

**Primary lymphoid organs** (where lymphocytes form or mature): red bone marrow and thymus. **Secondary lymphoid organs** (where lymphocytes meet antigens): lymph nodes, spleen, tonsils, adenoid, Peyer's patches and appendix.

## Functions

1. **Fluid balance:** collects excess interstitial fluid and returns it to the blood.
2. **Fat absorption:** **lacteals** (lymphatic capillaries in the small-intestine villi) absorb dietary fats.
3. **Immunity (immune defense):** lymphocytes recognize foreign material and neutralize or remove it.
4. **Filtering:** lymph nodes filter foreign particles and microorganisms from lymph; the spleen filters blood.

**Lymphatic duties vs blood duties:** filtering, immunity, fluid balance and fat absorption belong to the lymphatic system; delivering oxygen and nutrients and removing wastes belong to the blood.

## Immunity

**Immunity** is the ability to recognize foreign materials and neutralize or remove them without harming the body.

- **Antigen:** any substance that provokes antibody formation.
- **Antibody:** Y-shaped protein (made by B-cells) that inactivates an antigen.

| Route | Type | Produced by | Lasts |
|-------|------|-------------|-------|
| **Natural active** | Active (self-made) | Recovery after an infection | Long-lasting |
| **Artificial active** | Active (self-made) | A vaccine | Long-lasting |
| **Natural passive** | Passive (received) | Placenta and breast milk | Short-lasting |
| **Artificial passive** | Passive (received) | Injected antibody-containing serum | Short-lasting |
